# Lettr homepage: research and information architecture

Research date: 2026-09-21. This is a story and content recommendation, not a visual design or implementation brief. Competitor pages and reference sites are time-sensitive; the linked pages were reviewed for this document. Lettr capability checks use the current routes and the source map in [product-positioning.md](product-positioning.md). Older statements in `lettr-positioning-framework.md` are strategy, not a live feature or pricing specification.

## Decision in one paragraph

Tell the story of **one email platform for SaaS** through the actual handoff between two jobs: developers connect reliable sending, and product or marketing teammates create and change email without developer tickets. Show transactional and marketing as distinct sending streams inside one account and toolkit. Prove this with one understandable product moment before expanding to audience targeting, SaaS use cases, and delivery controls. Keep seven sections. Use Descript as the dominant _storytelling_ reference, Clerk for authentic technical/product proof, and Butter for a small number of spacious feature treatments. Translate their mechanisms into Lettr's own square geometry, Zalando Sans SemiExpanded, Manrope, DM Mono, blush/ink/white surfaces, and pink accent. Do not inherit any of their palettes or section counts.

## 1. What Lettr can credibly say

The intended buyer is a developer, technical founder, or tech lead at a small SaaS team. A product or marketing teammate becomes a daily user after integration. Lettr currently describes [REST API, SMTP, SDKs, webhooks, and logs](../../src/routes/email-api/+page.svelte) alongside [Audiences and Campaigns](../../src/routes/email-marketing/+page.svelte) and a [Topol-powered visual editor](../../src/routes/platform/templates/+page.svelte). Its [campaign launch article](../../src/routes/blog/introducing-lettr-marketing-audiences-and-campaigns/+page.svelte) describes one pool of **marketing contacts**, lists, segments, topics, subscription status, and campaign reporting. The [pricing route](../../src/routes/pricing/+page.svelte) separates transactional volume pricing from marketing contact pricing and offers a combined purchase path.

The strongest supported benefit is a shared platform and workflow, not a claim that every transactional recipient automatically becomes one universal customer record. Likewise, one platform does **not** mean one sending domain or reputation: Lettr's own [domain-separation article](../../src/routes/blog/separate-transactional-and-marketing-email/+page.svelte) recommends separate subdomains for transactional and marketing traffic. “High deliverability” should be demonstrated through authentication, domain controls, suppression/monitoring, and logs, without an inbox-placement guarantee or invented rate. The site's current [deliverability route](../../src/routes/platform/deliverability/+page.svelte) provides the deeper explanation.

The current homepage has ten sections: hero, proof strip, Devs/Teams switch, developer features, editor, use-case inbox, campaigns, pricing calculator, consultation CTA, and FAQ. The owner's [homepage critique](homepage-critique.md) already identifies clutter from simultaneous UI, repeated labels, many small feature points, and long proof captions. The recent component revisions improve individual sections, but the page still repeats the unification promise and dev/team story in several places. The architecture below starts from the visitor's questions instead of preserving that order.

**Competitive conclusion:** The plausible distinctive combination is Lettr's _specific developer-to-teammate workflow_, SaaS-only examples, EU-first setup, and email infrastructure heritage. The current FAQ comparison in `src/lib/components/FAQSection.svelte` should be fact-checked before it is reused; its Resend editor and Postmark broadcast claims conflict with their current offerings.

## 3. Exactly three final website references

I looked beyond email at [Linear](https://linear.app/) ([Refero](https://styles.refero.design/style/90ce5883-bb24-4466-93f7-801cd617b0d1), [Mobbin section](https://mobbin.com/sites/sections/f8b93019-f62b-4e58-8f0d-e577baa204f3)), [Retool's Mobbin section](https://mobbin.com/sites/sections/dcbd55be-58cb-4e0e-9b70-cb4d184fa30a), [Attio](https://attio.com/), [Stripe](https://stripe.com/), [Mercury](https://mercury.com/), [Figma](https://www.figma.com/), and [Webflow](https://webflow.com/). Linear and Retool can visualize a shared system, but the inspected examples use stacked or overlapping abstractions and tiny UI that would make Lettr's simpler message harder to grasp. Attio, Stripe, and Webflow have excellent product breadth and proof, but their current homepages serve larger product catalogs. The three references below solve Lettr's immediate communication problems more directly.

### 1. Descript — dominant storytelling reference

[Original website](https://www.descript.com/) · [Refero style reference](https://styles.refero.design/style/fe955d4a-c56d-4ab0-a6b3-8d985ab9570c)

- **Why relevant:** Descript makes a complicated creative workflow understandable as one coherent journey: record, edit, refine, share, multiply. The homepage centers the product action, rather than expecting a feature list to explain it.
- **Problem solved:** It gives visitors a mental model first and reveals capabilities in the order they become useful. The recognizable editor carries more meaning than abstract feature icons.
- **Useful moments:** Its opening product promise and “whole workflow” sequence; readable product scenes tied to each stage; a secondary use-case selector for visitors who want detail. The Refero page confirms a strong type and surface hierarchy around product UI.
- **Borrow:** One short through-line across the page and a few legible states of the _same_ object. For Lettr, a SaaS email can move from template to API event or audience to sent message. The visitor should feel continuity, even as the section composition changes.
- **Do not copy:** Descript's burgundy/coral palette, serif editorial typography, AI positioning, five-stage literal structure, extensive feature catalog, or constant video motion.
- **Lettr translation:** Use Lettr's blush canvas and square white/dark product surfaces. Keep a single email/template as the recurring object; use DM Mono only where real API or delivery data earns it. Motion should reveal a relationship, with a complete static state for reduced-motion visitors.

### 2. Clerk — technical credibility and progressive product proof

[Original website](https://clerk.com/) · [Refero style reference](https://styles.refero.design/style/ed10ae04-24ec-4e42-9bf2-ea12a4b58d67)

- **Why relevant:** Clerk sells infrastructure to developers while showing the interface that the rest of a team or end user experiences. It moves from a category claim to actual component examples rather than leaving the product abstract.
- **Problem solved:** Its component explorer lets a visitor choose a capability and see a concrete UI result. It proves technical depth without demanding that every capability be visible at once.
- **Useful moments:** The hero's “more than authentication” category expansion; the component selector and live previews; visible code/component labels adjacent to real UI; trust logos near the top.
- **Borrow:** A restrained choice that changes one large, believable Lettr product stage. A developer can view the trigger/API state and a teammate can view the editable email state, but the relationship between them must remain explicit.
- **Do not copy:** Clerk's violet glass, soft radii, atmospheric gradients, 3-column bento area, long component inventory, or an auto-advancing interaction that hides the essential story.
- **Lettr translation:** Keep Lettr's square geometry, hairline borders, pink active signal, and dark terminal where code is shown. The interaction should show a real Lettr-supported action and have a readable touch/keyboard state. The static fallback must communicate both roles.

### 3. Butter — spacious independent feature proof

[Mobbin feature section](https://mobbin.com/sites/sections/3e1db400-2e9f-477c-bdc7-f9209d245ca5)

- **Why relevant:** The owner has specifically endorsed this section's large graphic per idea, generous space, and short support copy. It is a useful complement after the central Lettr workflow is understood.
- **Problem solved:** Two separate capabilities remain memorable because each has one headline and one visual that actually explains it, rather than six equal-weight cards.
- **Useful moment:** The paired feature canvases: top-left claim, large centered product graphic, short low-priority support line. The Mobbin reference is a static desktop capture; mobile and interaction behavior are unverified.
- **Borrow:** Use at most two large visual proofs when the ideas are truly independent, likely sending controls and audience targeting or delivery visibility.
- **Do not copy:** Rounded gray cards, graphic effects, tiny captions, arbitrary abstract art, or the repeated two-card pattern throughout the entire homepage.
- **Lettr translation:** Square panels in white or continuous dark Lettr surfaces, one readable UI graphic per message, generous negative space, and pink used as a functional signal. This is a local composition reference, not the page's overall architecture.

**Hierarchy among references:** Descript determines narrative continuity and pacing. Clerk informs how a real product action and technical proof are revealed. Butter informs one limited feature composition. No blended visual style is proposed.

## 4. Recommended homepage story

The visitor should first understand the category and the cost of splitting SaaS email across tools. Next, the page establishes the accurate mental model: two kinds of email in one workspace, with separate sending domains where appropriate. Then it proves the developer-to-teammate handoff on a concrete message. Only after that should it widen to campaign targeting and SaaS lifecycle use cases. Operational trust and EU infrastructure answer the risk question near the decision point. The final step makes starting or switching feel tractable and offers the right route for self-serve and technical evaluation.

The page needs **seven sections**. The nav/footer are global chrome, not sections. Product and pricing details remain available through deeper pages. On mobile, the core message of each section must be visible without using a hidden tab or animation.

## 5. What to remove or move from the present homepage

- **Remove the hero announcement and three badge bullets as competing hero elements.** The category, tension, product proof, and signup action need first attention. A single EU cue can reappear in trust.
- **Remove the generic dithered-envelope scene as the main proof of “one platform.”** It can survive only if later design work makes it express the relationship between streams or the developer/team handoff.
- **Do not keep the standalone heritage statistic strip in its present position.** Topol/Ecomail figures may support section 6 after source and framing are checked, but they are not Lettr customer counts.
- **Combine the current Devs/Teams switch, developer feature grid, and editor section into one primary handoff story plus a small amount of operational proof.** The developer page and templates page can carry the full API, domain, webhook, log, sync, and localization detail.
- **Replace the six-row inbox and separate campaigns feature-card band with sections 4 and 5 above.** Remove invented company names, timings, delivery speeds, and statuses that can look like measured results. Use representative examples clearly as examples.
- **Move the transactional pricing slider and two plan cards to the pricing page.** On the homepage, a verified free-entry cue and a pricing link are enough. A calculator focused only on transactional volume interrupts the unified product story.
- **Fold the consultation CTA into the final decision section.** A separate “talk to an expert” section adds another stop immediately before conversion.
- **Move the long FAQ to deeper product, pricing, and docs pages or reduce it to only genuinely unresolved objections.** Do not publish the existing competitor-comparison answer until its Resend and Postmark claims are corrected against current offerings.

## 6. Questions to resolve before visual design

1. **What exactly is shared?** Confirm whether the visual editor, templates, analytics, billing, and account permissions are common across transactional and marketing. Confirm whether transactional recipients ever become marketing contact records, and by what explicit action.
2. **What is the precise handoff?** Can a teammate edit a transactional template used by an API call and publish it without a code change? What approvals/version behavior is live? Capture a real product path or approved UI screenshots.
3. **What marketing behavior is live?** Verify campaign scheduling, segmentation triggers, use of product events, and whether “onboarding sequences” or automations are actually available. The homepage should not imply a journey builder from examples alone.
4. **Which trust evidence is approved?** Validate EU hosting wording and legal claims, historical Topol/Ecomail numbers, Lettr customer logos/testimonials if any, and any operational metrics. Decide whether heritage is expressed as a small credential or omitted.
5. **What is the best first action for the developer-led buyer?** Choose between signup, a live product tour, first-send docs, or a deliberate primary/secondary pair; review actual conversion data if available.
6. **What is the switch path?** Can we accurately promise SMTP drop-in, staged migration, contact import, or hands-on assistance? Confirm the supported steps before section 7 is written.
7. **How should transactional and marketing domains be described?** Align the homepage, marketing route, FAQ, and launch article on “one platform, separate sending domains/reputations” before new public copy is created.

These questions do not block information architecture. They determine the final proof and wording inside the seven-section story.
